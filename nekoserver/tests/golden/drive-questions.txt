===== A first run: the list, then the app SSD, the media HDD and the USB backup drive =====
Everything connected to this PC:
  1) nvme0n1: 477 GB NVMe SSD, WD SN770
       Can be the app SSD or the media HDD.
  2) sda1: 466 GB SSD, Samsung SSD 870, named "apps"
       ext4, UUID ssd-0001; not open; about 1 % full
       Can be the app SSD, the media HDD or the USB backup drive.
  3) sdb1: 1.8 TB HDD, WDC WD20EZRZ, named "media"
       ext4, UUID hdd-0001; open at /run/media/neko/media; about 40 % full, holds files
       Can be the app SSD, the media HDD or the USB backup drive.
  4) sdc1: 29 GB USB drive, SanDisk Ultra, named "BACKUP"
       exFAT, UUID USB0-0001; not open
       Can be the USB backup drive.
  5) sdd1: 15 GB USB drive, Cruzer Blade, named "stick"
       ext4, UUID small-0001; not open; about 3 % full
       Can be the USB backup drive.
  6) sde: 932 GB HDD, ST1000DM010
       Cannot be used: it has no filesystem yet. To use it, format it as ext4 yourself with the Disks app (GNOME) or KDE Partition Manager, or in a terminal with  sudo mkfs.ext4 /dev/sde  (this erases what is on it; setup never formats a drive).
  7) sdf: 3.6 TB network disk (iSCSI), Synology LUN, named "nas-media"
       ext4, UUID nas-0001; the NAS disk 0x6001405abc; not open
       Can be the media HDD or the USB backup drive.
  8) nvme1n1p1: 233 GB NVMe SSD, KINGSTON SNV2S250G
       xfs, UUID nvme-0002; not open
       Can be the app SSD, the media HDD or the USB backup drive.
  9) sdg1: 238 GB SSD, Crucial MX500, named "Windows"
       NTFS (Windows), UUID WIN0-0001; not open
       Cannot be used: it is formatted NTFS (Windows), which Linux apps can't keep their files on. To use it, copy its files elsewhere first, then format it as ext4 yourself with the Disks app (GNOME) or KDE Partition Manager, or in a terminal with  sudo mkfs.ext4 /dev/sdg1  (this erases what is on it; setup never formats a drive).
The short names (sdb1, nvme0n1p2) are Linux's own and can change at the next start. NekoServer remembers a drive by its UUID, which you never have to type.
Setup never formats a drive and changes nothing by showing this list.

Number of the drive for your apps, photos and music (the app SSD) (Enter for 1, the recommended one): [1]
Drive 1 (nvme0n1: 477 GB NVMe SSD, WD SN770) is the app SSD. Nothing on it is changed by choosing it.
It is the disk Linux runs from: NekoServer keeps this in a folder of it (/var/lib/nekoserver). If that disk fails, Linux and what is kept there are lost together, and a disk that fills up stops Linux too. A drive of its own is the safer choice.
Now choose the drive for your movies, shows, Nextcloud files and the backups (the media HDD). It needs at least 50 GB free, so a drive of 54 GB or more (500 GB or more is recommended).
  1) nvme0n1: 477 GB NVMe SSD, WD SN770  (recommended)
Number of the drive for your movies, shows, Nextcloud files and the backups (the media HDD) (Enter for 1, the recommended one): [1]
Drive 1 (nvme0n1: 477 GB NVMe SSD, WD SN770) is the media HDD. Nothing on it is changed by choosing it.
It is the disk Linux runs from: NekoServer keeps this in a folder of it (/var/lib/nekoserver). If that disk fails, Linux and what is kept there are lost together, and a disk that fills up stops Linux too. A drive of its own is the safer choice.
The backups are then on the same disk as what they copy. They still help after a mistake or a broken app, but not when that disk fails: an offline backup on a removable drive is what covers that.

===== A PC with no drive that can be used =====
No drive here can be the app SSD (your apps, photos and music). NekoServer never formats a drive by itself, and never takes a drive that is in use elsewhere.
  - drive 1 (nvme0n1: 477 GB NVMe SSD, WD SN770): it has too little room: 5 GB free, and the app SSD needs at least 20 GB there
  - drive 2 (sde: 932 GB HDD, ST1000DM010): it has no filesystem yet. To use it, format it as ext4 yourself with the Disks app (GNOME) or KDE Partition Manager, or in a terminal with  sudo mkfs.ext4 /dev/sde  (this erases what is on it; setup never formats a drive)
Then run  sudo bash install.sh  again. Nothing was changed.
